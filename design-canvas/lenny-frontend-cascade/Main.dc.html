Our activation rate dropped after we added a second onboarding step. What do operators say about this?

Operators generally treat added onboarding friction as a real activation risk unless the extra step visibly earns its place. A couple of consistent threads:

Front-load value, not setup. Guests describe measuring "time to first value" per step and cutting any step that delays it without a clear payoff — a second step is fine if it's the moment the product actually clicks, risky if it's just more configuration.

Instrument the step in isolation. Rather than reading the aggregate activation number, operators pull step-level drop-off first — it usually isolates whether the regression is the new step itself or something else that shipped alongside it.

"...the teams that get onboarding right are obsessive about time-to-value per step, not steps-to-completion. A step is allowed to exist if it's the step where the product actually clicks for the user..."

what about B2B?

For B2B specifically, guests draw a sharper line: onboarding steps tied to team setup (inviting teammates, connecting a data source) are treated as investment the user is willing to make if activation happens after, since B2B activation is usually a team behavior, not a solo one.

"...in B2B, a setup step that gets a second teammate into the product is usually worth the friction, because your real activation event is a team behavior, not a single login. The mistake is friction that doesn't lead anywhere collaborative..."
